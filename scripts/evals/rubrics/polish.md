# Visual polish (0-5)

Score the overall craft and finish of the canvas: would this pass as professionally designed work of its kind (deck slide, social post, poster, banner, or document page)? This is theme-agnostic; both the light and dark themes can look equally polished.

- The canvas feels intentional and finished, not like a default template dump.
- Decoration (dot grid, soft glow, accent rules, icon circles, avatars, QR card)
  is used tastefully to support the content, never distracting or cluttered.
- Type sizing and weight create a pleasing rhythm; line lengths are comfortable;
  nothing is awkwardly large or cramped.
- Color accents (green, occasional gold) are placed deliberately to guide the eye,
  in the right proportion for the theme.
- Details are clean: crisp edges, even corner radii, aligned icons, no stray
  artifacts, consistent treatment across repeated elements (cards, stats, people).
- One primary framing move, not a stack of them. A card inside a card inside a
  bordered panel makes the content feel boxed in; enclosure should be earned, with
  whitespace and alignment doing the grouping wherever a border is not needed.
- No micro-clutter: pills, badges, tiny meta rows, and decorative micro-labels
  that carry no fact from the brief. Every small label should be information, not
  texture.
- Rules and hairlines organize real content. Grid lines, crosshairs, or a border
  on every row of a list drawn purely to make the page "feel designed" read as
  filler; pick one separator idiom and use it sparingly.
- Comparison figures are not drawn as filled-track progress bars. A number with a
  small mark reads cleaner than a large empty track with a partial fill.
- Corner radii are a system, not a single value applied everywhere: inner elements
  can sit tighter than the containers around them.

5 = polished, deliberate, gallery-quality within the house style. 3 = competent but with a few rough or generic touches. 0 = visually rough, cluttered, or unfinished.

Return "Unknown" if there is too little on the canvas to judge its craft.
